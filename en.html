INTRODUCTION: THE WORLD AFTER THE END

The "Metro 2033" universe, created by Dmitry Glukhovsky, is not just a post-apocalyptic thriller, but a deep social and political allegory. The Moscow Metro, having become the last refuge for the remnants of humanity after a nuclear war, has transformed into a microcosm of human history and sociology.

Deprived of the sky above their heads, people could not rid themselves of their nature. Instead of uniting in the face of radiation, mutants, and a constant lack of resources, the survivors divided once again along ideological, economic, and racial lines. Stations turned into dwarf states, and tunnels into dangerous borders.

“He who has enough courage and patience to stare into the darkness his whole life, shall be the first to see a flicker of light in it.”

HANZA (THE COMMONWEALTH OF THE RING)

IDEOLOGY: RADICAL CAPITALISM AND TRADE OLIGARCHY

Hanza is the richest, most powerful, and technologically advanced faction in the Metro. By taking control of the Ring Line, they monopolized trade and transit between the other branches. Their politics are built on harsh pragmatism: there is no place for high ideals here if they do not generate profit.

MILITARY MIGHT

A professional mercenary army. Excellent weaponry, access to pre-war depots, and their own industry. Regular, well-paid troops.

POPULATION

Around 15,000 – 20,000 people (the largest faction by number of citizens).

Through Hanza, Glukhovsky illustrates the modern consumer society and triumphant capitalism, where human life has a clear equivalent in currency, and morality retreats before economic expediency.

WHY DID PEOPLE FOLLOW THEM?

A thirst for stability and comfort. Those who wanted a "normal life" flocked to Hanza: electricity at the stations, the opportunity to earn money, and relative safety. People chose Hanza for its predictability and economic benefits, even if it meant being part of a soulless corporate machine ready to throw you out for debts.

THE RED LINE

IDEOLOGY: NEO-COMMUNISM AND STALINISM

Nostalgia for the "great past" of the USSR led to the creation of a totalitarian state on the Sokolnicheskaya line. Here, the Party rules, and there is a reign of scarcity, omnipresent propaganda, and fear of the secret services. The Red Line's politics are aggressive: they seek to forcefully impose their ideology throughout the Metro.

MILITARY MIGHT

A massive conscript army with low individual training. Reliance on sheer numbers, the fanaticism of commissars, and machine-gun blocking detachments.

POPULATION

Over 15,000 people (constantly growing through the forced annexation of stations).

Through the Red Line, Glukhovsky demonstrates the tragedy of totalitarianism and clinging to the ghosts of the past. It is a metaphor for how blind faith in an ideological utopia turns people into expendable material for the Party, and how the deceptive sense of community and order becomes a justification for repression and control.

WHY DID PEOPLE FOLLOW THEM?

A sense of community and nostalgia. For many survivors, the "Red idea" was their only connection to a great past. People followed the Line because it offered equality (albeit in poverty) and a clear meaning to life—the struggle for a brighter future. It provided a sense of protection against loneliness in the dark tunnels.

THE FOURTH REICH

IDEOLOGY: NEO-NAZISM AND GENETIC PURITY

Emerging at the intersection of three lines, the Reich built its politics on xenophobia. Under conditions of radiation and mutation, they elevated the concept of "purity of the human race" to an absolute. Their policy is one of genocide against anyone with physical anomalies.

MILITARY MIGHT

Supreme discipline and morale. The Reich's stormtroopers are among the most dangerous soldiers in the Metro. Their lack of numbers is compensated for by heavy weaponry.

POPULATION

Around 2,000 – 3,000 people (strict genetic culling limits the population).

Through the Fourth Reich, Glukhovsky illustrates the extreme degree of xenophobia and paranoia born from the fear of an invisible threat. It is an exploration of how absolute terror of external changes and biological transformation can turn a society into a fascist machine, destroying humanism for the sake of an illusory "purity of the species."

WHY DID PEOPLE FOLLOW THEM?

Fear of the "other" and the need for order. In an environment where radiation alters human bodies, the Reich offered a simple logic: "we are human, the rest are enemies." People followed them out of terror of mutations, seeking protection in strict discipline and a force that promised to preserve the human species at any cost.

POLIS

IDEOLOGY: TECHNOCRACY AND MERITOCRACY

The cultural and spiritual capital of the Metro. Polis aims to preserve the knowledge, art, and scientific achievements of the old world. The political structure of Polis is unique—it resembles the Indian caste system: Brahmins (scientists), Kshatriyas (warriors), Vaishyas (merchants), and Shudras (workers).

MILITARY MIGHT

The internal guard (Kshatriyas) is excellently trained, but in the event of a serious threat, Polis relies on the might of the Sparta Order.

POPULATION

Around 1,500 people (strictly limited migration).

Through Polis, Glukhovsky portrays civilization's last attempt to preserve humanity, intellect, and the heritage of the old world amidst primordial chaos. It is a symbol of an intellectual sanctuary, where the desire to safeguard culture coexists with hidden academic snobbery, caste isolation, and an inability to defend itself without external military force.

WHY DID PEOPLE FOLLOW THEM?

The drive to preserve humanity and knowledge. Those for whom survival was impossible without culture and science flocked to Polis. People chose this faction to avoid turning into beasts, believing that it was the knowledge of the old world that would someday help them return to the surface.

THE SPARTA ORDER

IDEOLOGY: MILITARY ORDER AND THE DEFENSE OF HUMANITY

The Order is not a state. It is an independent military-religious organization of elite fighters. Their policy is to intervene only when there is a threat to the existence of the entire Metro as a whole. They stand outside the politics of the stations, acting as the special forces of humanity.

MILITARY MIGHT

The absolute elite. Access to the best pre-war weaponry, night vision devices, and armored suits. One Spartan is worth a dozen ordinary soldiers.

POPULATION

Around 100 – 200 fighters (the Order is highly exclusive).

Through the Sparta Order, Glukhovsky reveals the ideal of selfless service and chivalry, contrasted with the petty internal feuds of the Metro. It is an image of a rare force capable of protecting people from external monsters, but simultaneously—an illustration of the tragedy of idealists, forced to blindly follow the orders of high command and become pawns in other people's political games.

WHY DID PEOPLE FOLLOW THEM?

The search for a higher duty. The best warriors, disillusioned with the political games of the stations, joined the Order. For them, Sparta was an opportunity to serve not leaders, but all of humanity, fighting real monsters. It was the path of the warrior, where honesty, brotherhood, and personal valor were valued.

THE INVISIBLE WATCHERS

IDEOLOGY: TOTAL CONTROL AND ISOLATION

The invisible government, revealed in the novel "Metro 2035". These are the remnants of the country's highest leadership, sheltering in the secret bunkers of "Metro-2". They secretly control the entire Metro, maintaining artificial conflicts so that people fear each other and do not attempt to contact survivors in the outside world.

MILITARY MIGHT

They have no direct armies but utilize the secret services of Hanza and the Red Line. Their main weapon is a network of electronic "jammers".

POPULATION

A small group of party elite and bunker personnel.

Through the Invisible Watchers, Glukhovsky illustrates the cynical nature of totalitarian power, ready to artificially sustain fear, scarcity, and war to maintain its own control. It is a metaphor for an information dome and a distorted reality, where the ruling elite sacrifices the future of all humanity by blocking communication with the outside world in order to remain the sole source of order in an isolated society.

WHY DID THE LEADERS FOLLOW THEM?

Power and the fear of the outside world. The faction leaders followed the Watchers because they guaranteed the preservation of their control. The Watchers convinced them that the world outside was dead, and only eternal war within the Metro would allow them to keep the population in check and prevent anarchy.

CONCLUSION: THE ILLUSION OF CHOICE

The examined ideologies are merely different walls of the exact same trap. As long as people divide stations and resources in the dark, they remain hostages to their own fears. The true catharsis of Artyom's story arrives when he realizes: the best choice is not participating in the endless war of factions, but overcoming the Metro itself. Emerging to the surface becomes an act of supreme freedom—a refusal to be part of a system that prefers slow decay in the tunnels to the chance of a new dawn.
